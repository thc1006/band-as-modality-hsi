Operational Earth-observation pipelines increasingly gate on deep segmentation models, and a large literature measures how well they retain accuracy under distribution shift. We show accuracy-robustness is not enough: a conformal certificate calibrated before deployment and reused under shift can be actively misleading. Using split conformal risk control on Sentinel-2 cloud segmentation (CloudSEN12), under the operational L1C->L2A atmospheric-correction shift a clean-calibrated threshold's confidently-wrong (joint) risk rises from 8.5% to 27.8% +/- 0.8 against a 10% target -- a +19-point rise the calibration statistic it minimises never reports, because that statistic stays below target by construction. The failure is not an artefact of one processor (a second, algorithmically different processor, ACOLITE, breaks it equally at 29.6%), one scene set (it replicates on held-out validation scenes at 28.0%), or one operating point (target risks 5-20%); covariate-shift weighted conformal does not repair it. We trace the breach to a stale input-normalization contract and find that re-normalizing each product with its own unlabelled statistics restores empirical control (10.8% at clean-like coverage), without target labels. The failure is conditional on the kind of shift and model-dependent: a channel-adaptive foundation model is far more reliable (9.9%), and within a matched convolutional family a larger receptive field attenuates the breach. The exchangeable unit must be the scene-connected component, with two-way cluster-robust standard errors. To our knowledge this is the first measurement of conformal-certificate failure for Sentinel-2 optical segmentation under a real atmospheric-correction shift.
